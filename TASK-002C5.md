## Redline: actual overcap with a control problem

There is one real reserve, not a hidden effective-RPM bonus. Normal cap is 1.0;
active Redline I permits **1.12**, II/Breakneck **1.18**, Runaway **1.24**. All
gains enter the real source ledger; ending overclock accounts for any vented
excess. HUD numeric RPM can exceed 9000 and explicitly labels OVERCLOCK.

Burst can engage at 13% RPM. The ordinary Burst cost remains; Redline adds
2.5% / 4.5% activation cost. Its normal active window is 3.2 seconds. Deliberate
motion above 135 world units/s, sufficient input, no braking and a non-edge
position can earn 4.5% RPM/s with a **14% activation quota**. The separate
motion bucket caps at 15% and refills 2.5%/s. Passive/AFK movement does not earn
this. Meaningful moving contacts have their own cooldown and target debounce,
and share normal/small-body recovery budgets.

Motion and excess spin raise heat; heavy contacts add heat. Inactivity cools it.
Extra running drain is 0.3% plus 0.6% × heat per second. Heat and overcap delay
steering, add deterministic lateral deviation and worsen braking and wobble
recovery. Performance therefore creates a physical edge risk. Rank I supplies
the overclock fantasy immediately; II strengthens the same machine.

**Runaway** converts accepted aggressive contacts into bounded reclamation and
renewal. It replenishes a small finite motion quota, adds heat and physical
follow-through, and renews only up to 3.2 seconds remaining. Shared contact
cooldown is 0.65s and same-target debounce 1s. Small bodies remain under the
shared small recovery bucket; collision spam cannot create separate unlimited
income. Stop making useful contacts and overload expires.

**Breakneck** first builds a 4.8s overclock setup, long enough to use the next
ordinary Burst. At 32% heat or 2.5% excess RPM, that Burst pays 8% plus 75% of
excess spin and commits heading for 0.48s. It creates a real velocity launch,
strong contact weighting, target impulse and owner recoil. Hit aftermath spends
another 1.5%, adds wobble and reduces speed; a miss spends 3.5% with stronger
wobble/speed consequences. Tests and actual combat diagnostics include hits,
misses, accounted expenditure and real ring-outs.

## Clutch replaces draftable Second Wind

No revive, relaunch, arena reset, player replacement or full refill. Clutch arms
while the live machine is above 4.5% and at/below 28% RPM. It gives a 6s / 8s
window; controlled movement conserves expenditure and slightly improves
recovery. A useful full-rival contact requires pre-contact danger at/below 30%,
meaningful severity (0.22), motion (25 units/s), input, and cooldown/target
debounce. Each catch earns at most 3.5% / 5.5%, subject to ordinary reclamation
budgets and a **9% / 13% per-window quota**. Wobble decreases by only 0.04.
Rearming requires escaping above 36% RPM and a 9s cooldown. Defeated tops and
small-body farming cannot trigger catches.

Eligibility uses the dangerous RPM approaching the contact. Normal contact
reclamation runs first; checking only the resulting reserve had incorrectly
cancelled the bonus precisely when a useful hit was made. This was corrected
and tested, along with the live Rank II acquisition seam.

accepted standalone fixtures. It is absent from normal offers and starting

## Speed, drift and physical utility

High Gear I multiplies real acceleration by 1.35 and ceiling by 1.27. II uses
1.55 / 1.46 and retains more velocity through shallow corrections. In a real
90-frame movement comparison, baseline / I / II / Terminal / Flow measured
**226.79 / 294.58 / 345.68 / 430.92 / 352.78 units/s**. The final global modern
full-top safety cap is **480 units/s**, including Burst/contact/shear recoil.

Terminal Velocity uses the highest raw ceiling (1.95 acceleration / 1.82
ceiling), but hard steering explicitly spends accounted RPM and braking costs
more. Flow State has a lower raw ceiling, carries heading through smoother
corrections, reduces drag and conserves sustained movement. These branches
change how the fast machine is driven.

Orbit Drive's brake-turn slide retains forward inertia and physically carves
velocity toward the chosen turn. Actual sustained curvature builds charge;
charge increases momentum and reduces motion loss. Rank II deepens retained
flow. A 90-frame real movement test holds over 1.35s of drift, carries more than
1.8× ordinary braked momentum, and proves that the chosen direction becomes an
arc. There is no automatic orbit or gate rescue.

Crash Guard's initial heavy full-rival hit is unprotected. It then reduces
full-top collision cost to 68% / 48% for 1.15 / 1.65s, with a 3s trigger
cooldown. Small swarm-contact loss retains its existing shared economy rules.
Momentum Bank stores actual speed lost under controlled straight braking,
capped at 95 / 150 motion units, decays, and empties on a paid Burst release.
Drifting does not simultaneously manufacture that braking bank. Predator Line
stacks up to three meaningful contacts on one full rival; changing rival or
disengaging breaks focus. Crosscut's lateral input creates a priced, debounced
physical shear and opposite recoil; both remain under safety velocity bounds.

## Afterimage, Slipstream and assisted Ghost Circuit

Rank I now emits useful paid traces above 112 units/s, at 0.14s intervals and
0.08% RPM each. The trace's physical pressure can displace full and small
opponents. II lasts longer, pushes harder and rewards crossing an aged own
trace with a small momentum/handling surge. Trace costs remain real.
Slipstream makes this route reuse more immediate and stronger, with a cooldown
and aged-path requirement rather than rewarding continuous occupancy.

Ghost scans only a chronological connected route belonging to the same live
owner. A valid route shows two clamp sockets and a dashed bridge within 70px;
the next **paid visible emission** can snap a gap up to 38px. It requires aged
geometry, sufficient perimeter (180), area (1300) and bounds (32), meaningful
speed, and a 3.5s cooldown. Unrelated/distant traces, tiny loops, same-route
retriggering and invisible low-speed closing chords are rejected.

The completed loop energises its route and pushes enclosed targets, with
visible wobble and a short movement surge. Full rivals receive 88-unit pressure,
0.12 wobble and 1.2% RPM pressure; small bodies receive 110 / 0.20 / 1.8%.
Targets per closure are capped at eight, polygon points at 224, all live traces
at 72. Modern Ghost retains at most **40 own traces for 6s**.

Iteration found that a nominal 5.5s route lifetime was ineffective with a
28-segment cap: 0.14s emissions left roughly four seconds of geometry. Adding
the paid closing segment discarded the socket that the preview promised.
At the exact natural Vane seed421 tick 122.15s, a visible 22.42px socket became
45.22px after eviction and failed the 38px snap. Forty retained segments make
that same real paid tick close. This fixes route structure, rather than merely
inflating closure radius. Natural deliberate route controllers now close
circuits; slow or interrupted routes can still fail legitimately.

### Limits and next human decisions

- Automation validates mechanics and deterministic behavior, not satisfying
  controller feel, fair difficulty or final visual acceptance. The drift's
  Need for Speed-like momentum fantasy requires physical human judgement.
- Aggressive overclock samples often ring out; careful controlled overclock can
  remain alive at its ceiling. These input styles do not prove every death was
  caused by heat or that ring-out is unavoidable.
- Deliberately optimized repeated route play can be very strong. Slow Bastion
  without movement investment and disrupted paths can still fail. Judge the
  preview/closure reliability and repetition in actual mixed combat.
- First Rank II is reachable early; median first mutation is later than the
  historical C.3 sample. The enlarged pool, preferences and controllers differ,
  so this is a pacing question for humans, not a controlled causal comparison.
- Safe comeback-preference bots do not enter Clutch danger. The danger-specific
  samples establish earned catches and possible defeat; humans must judge how
  useful its window feels in an ordinary chosen build.
- Crash Guard and Predator Line emphasize full-rival brawls. Small swarms retain
  their shared recovery/collision budgets rather than opening new income loops.

  feedback may still change that decision. No starter class restriction exists.
