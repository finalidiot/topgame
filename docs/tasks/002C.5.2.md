## Data and physics architecture

`assets/data/parts_catalogue.json` is the runtime content source. Each entry has
stable ID, category, display name, rarity, description, mechanical identity,
trade-off, ordering, legacy flag, ratings, visual paths and physical parameters.
Ownership remains entirely in the existing collection save API. The catalogue
loader and tests check IDs against the existing three-category save protocol.

`scripts/part_physics.gd` composes bounded coefficients without part-ID branches
in the solver. Reusable properties cover mass, contact reach/profile, alignment,
impact/recoil/shock, repeated pressure, inertia, wobble recovery, acceleration,
control, drag, sideways scrub, braking, centre seeking, curved motion, orbit,
RPM cost, threshold/rhythmic grip and wall rebound. Part contact memory is bounded
to twelve targets and decays; pressure is capped. Shape profiles approximate
the authored rim rather than providing pixel-perfect concave rigid bodies.

`scripts/battle.gd` opts new assemblies into those hooks. Original-only assemblies
keep their old arithmetic. Motion slices bound high-speed contact travel for new
assemblies. New Blade spin phase maps authored planar geometry to world u/v and
the isometric renderer; all layers share the existing contact-ground alignment.
Ratchet height affects both physical contact and preview/runtime assembly height.

Temporary Run powers use the machine's resulting movement and collision state.
There are no part/power combo bonuses. Protected C5 power, roster, RPM economy,
RunContext, starter and Threat Director scripts remain byte-identical to the
starting snapshot. Original-only seeded Runs and physics are compared directly
against actual historical source, including RPM ledgers and director/power state.

`tools/parts/create_parts_catalogue_data.py` is a deliberate one-off authoring
helper based on the exact parent definitions. The JSON remains authoritative;
rerunning the authoring helper replaces it and should only be done intentionally.

## Simulation, regression and build evidence

Final measurements and validation are recorded after the integration freeze in
the external manifests and compact versioned result linked below. The study
uses stratified assemblies and fixed seeds rather than every full fight among
1,089 possible builds. Contact/motion probes are labelled diagnostics; the review
movie contains real combat driven by legal inputs, not injected contacts or RPM.

The retained and new regression batch passed **40 suites / 198,301 counted
checks**, including continuous Run, RPM replay/economy, Threat Director, powers,
collection persistence/flow, starter ownership/physics, workshop, menus,
controller navigation, combat, swarm, escalation and source/presentation assets.
The actual player collection, backup and preferences hashes were unchanged.
The differential tests passed 127,776 historical physics comparisons, 13,446
parent Run state/ledger comparisons and all 48 direct legacy fixture cases.
New catalogue checks passed 8,945 assertions, including every legal construction,
all twenty new parts with at least one measured 10% physical contrast, sixty
starter/part swaps, profile alignment and new-part swarm/continuous-Run coverage.

The assembly study passed 576 safety assertions for 72 stratified assemblies
and 144 real 18-second bouts, with separate motion/contact/wall probes. Across
these samples, mean speed spans 29.45–164.47, peak speed 86.47–480, remaining
RPM 0.111–0.776, mean wobble 0–0.827, centre occupancy 2.13%–99.54% and wall
exposure 0–44.05%. Maximum observed low-speed wall lock was 0.15 seconds.
No NaN, runaway acceleration, instant unavoidable self ring-out or unlimited
RPM was detected in this coverage. Most fights reached the short horizon;
completed win fractions are unsuitable for a balance ranking.

Fourteen diagnostic objectives did not find a component dominated across all
measured trade-offs. This does not prove equal strength or exclude a mandatory
choice in skilled human play. Freewheel's reserve efficiency comes with low
authority; fast Skate builds expose themselves to walls; Claw and offset builds
have high wobble/control demands. Initial Sawtooth pressure had too little paid
contact benefit and was corrected within its own new definition. Defensive
Puck/Flex or Ballast/Tripod rigs are particularly efficient under centre control
and should receive focused human testing.

An additional six zero-input late-Run samples passed 18 assertions: all spun
out within a 300-second horizon. Puck/Ballast/Tripod lasted 245.85–261.67 seconds,
Puck/Flex/Tripod 288.10–299.68, and original Bastion 211.45–221.67. Active combat
reclamation was zero; bounded existing small-elimination credits were recorded
honestly. This diagnostic found longer unattended defence, but no stationary
unbeatable machine in the tested seeds. It does not alter provisional C5 RPM
rules. Separate 90-second new-part Run tests use centre feedback, not zero input.

Infrastructure tests passed 21 build-promotion guards, the workspace safety
suite, and two regression-summary parsing guards. Python tools compile, native
Aseprite parity passes, final Godot source import succeeds and `git diff --check`
passes. An initial runner summary falsely interpreted `checks=371 failures=0`;
the parser was corrected and guarded before rerunning the full batch.

Step timing is reported in the matrix as CPU solver measurement, not GPU frame
time or hardware-independent FPS. The final study observed 0.341 milliseconds
mean step time and a 34.083 millisecond maximum during concurrent diagnostics.
The isolated step mean is small, but those scheduling outliers do not establish
smooth rendered FPS or exclude a performance issue on other machines.

The prototype's historical 48-assembly/144-bout baseline remains its original
scope; separate catalogue checks cover all 1,089 legal constructions and new
individual mechanics. This avoids silently expanding a historical full-fight
test to thousands of bouts while retaining its comparison value.

The Windows pipeline exports exact clean committed HEAD with a fresh import,
embedded data, hashes, isolated packaged smoke and guarded promotion. It retains
the previous validated latest build and only promotes a passing candidate.
Stacked C5 checkpoint manifests must retain pending human acceptance.

Before promotion, `tools/parts/verify_packaged_catalogue.py` additionally runs
the actual candidate EXE with an isolated QA collection and checks all 31 owned
IDs, exact packaged JSON and all 42 component textures. Final export, packaged
smoke, supplementary catalogue inspection and promotion results live alongside
the exact SHA in the delivery manifest and final external checkpoint record.
These records are written after this source commit; a failed candidate is never
presented as a delivered Windows checkpoint.

The first supplemental checker attempted the editor-only `--script` entry point
against a release template. Its ownership check succeeded, but the external
asset script was ignored and timed out; that candidate was not promoted and
the real profile was unchanged. The checker now uses the compiled read-only
`scripts/parts_package_probe.gd` through scoped QA flags. It requires an isolated
collection and a fresh external manifest path, refuses overwrites, and exits
with an explicit inspection result. Normal gameplay does not run the probe.
Source CLI valid/refused cases and 253 package/path-safety assertions passed.
After this integration, six relevant UI/collection/controller/package suites
passed another 9,620 checks; the existing collection and collection-flow suites
also passed their targeted reruns. The final rebuilt release must pass the same
compiled inspection before guarded promotion.

## Remaining acceptance limits

Automated safety and contrast measurements do not establish universal balance
or whether a weird part is enjoyable. Short fights, fixed opponent policies and
diagnostic contact setups have limited dominance evidence. Shape contact uses
bounded rim approximations; visual recognition during busy encounters and
physical controller feel still need human testing. No claim of a mathematically
optimal catalogue or final C5 gameplay acceptance is made.

Review all parts together and report interchangeable, mandatory, simply worse,
visually unclear or particularly fun components. This checkpoint is intended to
let the human feel different machines before future distribution systems exist.
