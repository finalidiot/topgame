Two catalogue assumptions need care. Ordinary Crash Guard is triggered by an
opposing accepted contact severity>=.45, without a rival directional-approach
gate. It arms after that triggering collision; its1.15/1.65-second window
discounts subsequent contact loss by .68/.48. New received-hit branches can
accurately require actual incoming direction and solver force. Iron Comet I/II
consumes its charge on the next accepted opposing contact, without a hard-hit
gate. The new Wallbreaker directional/full-top timing therefore changes the
decision rather than merely increasing its old shove.

Clutch, Dead Centre, Impact Sink and Orbit Drive can help many builds, but source
does not establish them as universally optimal. Their active gates, finite
resources/shared recovery and seven-slot investment cost remain material.
Matched legal builds and explicit playing policies are needed to assess that
question. This task does not nerf them from broad utility alone.

## Four selections and rejected alternatives

Iron Comet is the only existing solid-wall setup family. Its pair can distinguish
one risky reflected-line attack from repeated controlled oblique wall routes.
Crash Guard currently offers a later-contact scalar discount; a brief aimed
counter and a single costly collision sacrifice deepen real defensive response.
Orbit Drive already earns a curve resource, so spending DRIVE versus preserving
it gives a genuine technique choice. Momentum Bank already stores paid lost
motion, making a larger straight commitment versus an angled redirect a useful
resource decision.

Clutch's extra emergency recovery would duplicate existing finite sustain and
risk disguising failure. More Wake/Chain pulses would overlap local separation
and Return Spring. Predator already distinguishes same-target pursuit. A new
Crosscut pair would overlap the selected Centrifuge tangent response. These are
deferred candidates, not removed abilities.
